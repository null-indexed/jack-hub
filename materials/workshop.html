The Workshop

Your space for rebuilding laptops, installing operating systems, rescuing scrap devices and modding. Pick one project at a time, finish it, log it, then pick the next.

Workshop rules

  • Your devices only, or ones the owner has said you can take apart. Write down who gave it to you.
  • Locked to someone else? If a device asks for another person's Apple ID or Google account, stop. That lock is there to make stolen devices useless. The fix is the owner removing it, never a bypass tool.
  • Swollen or damaged batteries: don't charge, press or puncture them. Keep them away from anything that burns and take them to battery recycling at the tip. Lithium fires are serious.
  • Never open power supply bricks, old CRT monitors or microwaves. They can hold a dangerous charge even when unplugged.
  • Unplug and remove the battery before opening a laptop. Touch something metal first to get rid of static.
  • Back up before you flash or wipe. Assume it might not work.
  • No pirated software, games or ROMs. Use free and open source software, or things you own.

How to use AI in the workshop

Paste an error message and ask what it means and where to look. Ask it to explain a step you don't understand. Then check against the official guide or iFixit before you do anything you can't undo. AI is wrong often enough with hardware that a blind copy can brick a device.

Every project, the same five steps

  1. Plan: what will "working" look like? What could go wrong?
  2. Photo before: take pictures as you take it apart, so you know how it goes back.
  3. Do it: one step at a time, with the guide open.
  4. Log it: what went wrong and how you fixed it is the most valuable bit.
  5. Teach it back: show Dad, or explain it to Max.

Jailbreaking and rooting old tablets

Jailbreaking (Apple) and rooting (Android) mean unlocking a device so you can change things the maker normally blocks. It's a great way to learn how operating systems work.

Before you start, know this: it can break a device for good, it ends any warranty, and it can make a device less secure. So only on old devices you own, never on your main phone, and never to get paid apps or games for free.

Dad's research on this is being added here. Until then, start by writing down the exact model and software version of each old tablet you have.

Links worth knowing

Where to look things up

  • iFixit: step by step repair guides with photos, for almost every device.
  • Linux Journey: learn the Linux command line, free.
  • Raspberry Pi projects: free guided builds.
  • LineageOS device list: check whether an old Android device is supported.
