How school and home fit together

School is loud and fast, so it's your first look at a topic, not the time you're meant to learn it all. Your job in class is just to get it into your notebook. Bring the notebook home. At home, where it's quiet, you go through it step by step: break it down, redraw it, put it into your hub and study guide, and use pictures and short videos to make it stick. That's where the learning happens.

The traffic lights

Look at one topic, close your book, and ask yourself one question: do I know this?

Red = I don't know it
Amber = I kind of know it
Green = I know it

Do the amber ones first. You're already halfway there, so one read of your book usually turns them green. Then the reds. Greens just need a quick look the night before.

Write R, A or G next to each topic on your science checklists.

Why lithium batteries keep burning under water

Lithium is in Group 1. Group 1 atoms have one electron in their outer shell, and they give it away very easily to become a positive ion (Li⁺). That's why they're so reactive. Positive means an electron has been lost, not taken.

A damaged battery heats up, and the heat makes it break down and heat up even more (this is called thermal runaway). Inside, some of the chemicals release their own oxygen as they break down, so the fire doesn't need air. Water can't smother it, because the oxygen in water is locked inside the H₂O molecules and isn't free to feed a fire. The fire is carrying its own supply.

Check in your book: how many electrons does lithium have, and how are they arranged? That's C1.2.
