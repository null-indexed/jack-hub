My test revision plan

For the Year 9 tests in November 2026. Made on 4 November 2026. Maths, English and science first. Ticks save on this device.

The big idea. "Revise everything we've learned this year" sounds huge, but it only means what's in your exercise books since September. That's about ten weeks of work, and you were there for all of it. Revision is reminding yourself, not learning it again.

Step 2: traffic light each section (1 minute each)

For each section on your list, close the book and try to say or scribble what it's about in one minute.

  • Green: I could explain it to a friend. Quick check only.
  • Amber: I remember some of it. Start here. These are the fastest marks, because one read of your book usually brings them back.
  • Red: I don't remember it, or it never made sense. Do these after the ambers, one small piece at a time.

You'll probably find more green and amber than you expect. That's normal, and it's evidence you know more than you think.

English

English is about practice more than facts. One short extract (a paragraph from any book at home) and one written paragraph about it is a full burst.

Leave it until last. If you want a quick practice, Google Sheets is free in a browser and works on a Mac, and the basics (SUM, IF, simple formulas, charts) are almost the same as Excel. Worth asking Mrs Cadman whether the test is on a computer or on paper.

Step 3: the weekly rhythm

Homework first every day, because it counts as revision. Then two or three bursts of 10 to 15 minutes, with a walk about in between. One section per burst. Then stop.

Two weeks of this covers every section on your list at least once, with chemistry and maths coming round most often. Move days around to fit homework and DT. If a test is in a few days, give that subject the first burst each day until then.

Each burst, step by step

  1. Pick one section from your list.
  2. Brain dump: book closed, write or draw what you remember.
  3. Read that section in your book twice.
  4. Make a card or page: key words and a labelled diagram.
  5. Look up only the bits you couldn't fill (CGP, Bitesize, Kerboodle).
  6. Change its traffic light colour.

More detail on How to revise.

In the test

  • Read each question twice. Underline what it's asking for.
  • Look at the marks. A 1 mark question needs one point, a 4 mark question needs four.
  • Go through once doing every question you can do fairly quickly. Put a dot next to the hard ones and come back. A question you come back to beats a question you never reach.
  • If you finish early, check the questions with a dot first.

You don't have to do everything. You have to do the next small thing. Pick one amber section and do one burst. That's today done.
