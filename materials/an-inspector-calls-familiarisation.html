An Inspector Calls: familiarisation

English Literature, AQA 8702 Paper 2 Section A. Deep study Year 10 Autumn. Read it before then.

Why read it early

It's short (three acts, one room, one evening) and it's a gripping read. Knowing the plot before Year 10 frees you up to focus on Priestley's message and methods in class.

Context in one paragraph

J.B. Priestley wrote it in 1945 and it was first performed that year. It's set in 1912. The audience knew what came after 1912 (the Titanic, two world wars). Keep asking: what would a 1945 audience think when the characters say confident things about the future?

Who's who (fill in as you go)

  • Arthur Birling, Sybil Birling, Sheila Birling, Eric Birling
  • Gerald Croft
  • Inspector Goole
  • Eva Smith / Daisy Renton (never seen on stage)
  • Edna, the maid

For each, note their link to Eva Smith and how they react to the Inspector.

Track as you read

  • Act 1, 2 and 3: three sentences each on what happens.
  • Who changes, and who doesn't? Split the characters into two groups.
  • The ending: what happens after the Inspector leaves, and what does the final phone call suggest?

Big ideas to notice

Responsibility, social class, gender, age (younger vs older generation), capitalism vs socialism.

Questions to think about

  1. What is the Inspector for? Is he a real policeman?
  2. Why does Priestley make Mr Birling wrong about so many things in Act 1?
  3. Which character do you think learns the most? Why?
