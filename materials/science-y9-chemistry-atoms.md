## Counting atoms in a formula

The method:

- Every element symbol starts with a capital letter (O, Na, Cl). A small letter belongs to the capital before it.
- A small number written low after a symbol tells you how many of that atom. No number means one.
- The same element can appear in more than one place in a formula. Count every place and add them up.

This last rule is the trap. In the session, the ethanol question (C₂H₅OH) was the one that caught us out: hydrogen appears twice.

Try these. For each one, write the number of atoms of each element and the total.

1. NH₃ (ammonia)
2. CO₂
3. H₂SO₄
4. C₂H₅OH (ethanol)
5. CH₃COOH
6. Ca(OH)₂ (hint: look up what the number after a bracket does)

## Self-test questions

1. An atom has atomic number 11 and mass number 23. How many protons, neutrons and electrons does it have? Which element is it?
2. Why is the mass number never smaller than the atomic number?
3. Describe one way Bohr's model improved on Rutherford's.
4. How many atoms in total are in one molecule of H₂SO₄?
5. Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 are isotopes. What is the difference between them?
