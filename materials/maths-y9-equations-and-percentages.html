Maths: percentages, simplifying and solving equations

AQA GCSE Maths (8300), Foundation. Built from Jack's Sparx homework session with Belinda on 3 November 2026.

Before you start: slow down

In the session you knew the method every time. The mistakes came from rushing. Belinda: "See? You know your stuff. When you give yourself time, you know your stuff really well."

For every question:

  1. Read it twice. Underline what it asks for.
  2. Write one step per line. Don't do two steps in your head at once.
  3. Check your answer makes sense. Put it back into the question if you can.

1. Percentages by building blocks

You don't need a calculator for most percentages. Build them from pieces you can find easily:

  • 10%: divide by 10
  • 5%: half of 10%
  • 1%: divide by 100
  • 50%: half
  • 25%: half of 50%, or a quarter

Then add the pieces. For example 15% is 10% plus 5%, and 35% is 10% + 10% + 10% + 5%.

Write which pieces you'll use before you calculate. Try these:

  1. 15% of 80
  2. 25% of 512
  3. 35% of 240
  4. 20% of £45
  5. A coat costs £60. It's reduced by 15%. What is the new price? (Two steps: find the 15%, then take it off.)
  6. Word problem: underline the starting amount, the percentage, and whether you need the part or the new total. Write those three things down before you start.

2. Simplifying expressions (collecting like terms)

Like terms have exactly the same letters with exactly the same powers.

  • 3f and 7f are like terms.
  • 3f² and 7f² are like terms.
  • 3f² and 7f are not like terms. f² and f are different, so they stay separate.
  • A number on its own (like + 7) only collects with other numbers on their own.
  • The sign in front of a term belongs to that term. Move it with the term.

Method: circle each set of like terms in a different colour, then add or subtract within each colour.

Try these:

  1. 5d − 3d + 8
  2. 7k + 2u − 3k + 5u
  3. 6v² + 4v + 2v²
  4. 3a² + 2a − a² + 4a − 5
  5. 15f² + 19f² + 7 + 4w

3. Solving linear equations

Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Undo the equation in reverse order: get rid of any adding or subtracting first, then multiplying or dividing.

Try these, one step per line:

  1. x + 7 = 19
  2. y − 2.6 = 4.1
  3. 2x − 4 = 12
  4. 3x + 5 = 26
  5. 4x − 9 = 15

4. Solving equations with x²

This is new, so here is the worked example from the session. Read each line and say what was done.

7x² = 28

x² = 4 (divide both sides by 7)

x = 2 or x = −2 (square root both sides)

Why two answers? 2 × 2 = 4 and also −2 × −2 = 4. You write it as x = ±2.

The steps, in order:

  1. Get the x² term on its own. If there's a number added or taken away, undo that first.
  2. Divide by the number in front of x².
  3. Square root both sides.
  4. Write the answer as ± (plus or minus).

Belinda's tip from the session: once you've seen it in the example, "You wouldn't even have to say I'm taking 25 away from this side. You just take it from that side. And then whatever's left, divide it."

Try these:

  1. 3x² = 48
  2. 6x² + 25 = 79
  3. 2x² + 7 = 57
  4. 5x² − 20 = 25
  5. x² − 31 = 50
