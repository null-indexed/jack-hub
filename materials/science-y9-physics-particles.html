Physics 1.1: The particle model and density

OCR Gateway Physics (J259), Year 9, Autumn.

What you need to be able to do

  • Use the particle model to explain why solids, liquids and gases have different densities
  • Calculate density
  • Describe how to measure the density of regular and irregular objects

The equation

Density = mass ÷ volume (ρ = m ÷ V)

Units: kg/m³ or g/cm³. You must be able to rearrange it to find mass or volume.

Required practical: measuring density

Work out the steps yourself before checking:

  • Regular object (a block): how do you find the volume?
  • Irregular object (a stone): how do you find the volume? Hint: a displacement can.
  • Liquid: what do you measure, and with what?

Self-test questions

  1. A block has mass 540 g and volume 200 cm³. What is its density?
  2. Explain using particles why ice floats on water. (This one is unusual, think carefully.)
  3. Why is a gas less dense than the same substance as a solid?
  4. A stone pushes 35 cm³ of water out of a displacement can and has mass 91 g. What is its density?
