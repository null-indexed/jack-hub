AI roadmap

A path you and Dad work through together. You pick what to explore inside each level. Move on when you can teach it back, not when a date says so.

How we use AI here

AI is your navigator, not your answer machine. Ask it where to look, to quiz you, or to explain a mistake after you've had a go yourself. Don't ask it to do the work for you: if it does the thinking, you don't learn anything.

Some AI tools have age limits. Claude is 18 and over, so anything on Claude happens together on Dad's account. Check the rules before signing up for anything new. Most of the tools on this page need no account at all.

The rhythm

One session a week together, about 45 minutes. Afterwards, add a few lines to your AI log: what you tried, what surprised you, and one sentence explaining it like you'd tell a friend. Tick things off below as you go (the ticks are saved on this device).

Level 1

What AI actually is

AI doesn't think. It spots patterns in loads of examples and makes a guess. This level is about seeing that for yourself.

Teach it back: explain to Dad why your model got confused, and what "training data" means.

Level 2

Using AI well, and catching it out

AI sounds confident even when it's wrong. Learning to check it is the most useful skill on this page.

Teach it back: show Dad one answer the AI got wrong, and how you proved it.

Level 3

How machines learn

Now build some small models yourself and see how the data shapes what they do.

Teach it back: draw (on paper is fine) how data goes in, a model gets trained, and guesses come out.

Level 4

Run AI on your own hardware

This is where your laptop skills come in. Small AI models can run offline on a machine you've rebuilt.

Teach it back: explain why your old laptop is slower at running a model, using your benchmark numbers. As a rough guide, 8 GB of RAM is a sensible minimum for small models.

Level 5

Build with AI

Use AI as a coding partner, not a ghost writer. The rule: if you can't explain a line, it doesn't go in.

Teach it back: walk Dad through your code line by line. Add the finished thing to Things I've built.

Level 6

The big questions

Pick one topic a week, find out about it, and argue it out with Dad over dinner.

Teach it back: write five lines in your log giving your own opinion, with one fact to back it up.
